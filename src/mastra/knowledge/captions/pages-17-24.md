## Owner Manual Page 17

**Content:** Finishing the MIG wire setup (steps 27–31): checking drive tension, closing the door, installing the Contact Tip and Nozzle. Then "Optional Spool Gun Setup" — a 6-step procedure for connecting the optional Spool Gun (sold separately), including cable polarity, wire feed control cable, and gas hose connections.

**Tables:** None.

**Diagrams & images:**
- **Drive tension check illustration (top right):** A VULCAN MIG Gun held 2–3" above a piece of wood, with wire arcing out and bending as it presses against the wood. Callout: "Incrementally increase tension until wire bends." and "2–3"". To the left, a line drawing of the wire feed mechanism with a curved arrow indicating clockwise tightening of the Feed Tensioner.
- **MIG Gun tip assembly drawing (middle right):** Cutaway/line drawing of the MIG Gun neck with labels "Nozzle", "Contact Tip", and "MIG Gun" — showing the Nozzle sliding over the Contact Tip at the gun's end.
- **Spool Gun connection diagram (lower right, large):** Isometric drawing of the welder front panel with door open, showing all spool gun connections. Labels and callouts: "Spool Gun" (the gun itself, lower left), "Spool Gun Cable Connector", "Wire Feed Control Cable", "Gas Hose", "Ground Clamp Cable in Negative Socket" (ground clamp shown at bottom), "Wire Feed Power Cable in Positive Socket", and "Spool Gun Gas Outlet" (on the welder front, right side). Shows the spool gun cable connector passing through the hole in the welder front into the wire feed socket.
- Right-edge section tabs visible: SAFETY, CONTROLS, WIRE (highlighted/active), TIG/STICK, WELDING TIPS, MAINTENANCE.
- JSON manifest lists no extracted image files for this page.

**Key facts:**
- Check drive tension by feeding wire against wood from 2–3 inches away; wire stops feeding after 3 seconds if there is no arc, so check tension for less than 3 seconds.
- If wire stops instead of bending: unplug the Welder, slightly tighten the Feed Tensioner clockwise, retry. If wire bends from feed pressure, tension is set properly.
- After tension check: turn OFF Power Switch and unplug Power Cord before proceeding.
- Contact Tip: slide over wire, thread clockwise into MIG Gun, tighten. Replace Nozzle; cut wire off at 1/2" from tip (1/2" stickout).
- Spool Gun setup polarity: Ground Clamp Cable → Negative (–) Socket; Wire Feed Power Cable → Positive (+) Socket. Twist cables clockwise all the way to lock.
- Spool Gun Cable Connector inserts through hole on Welder front into socket on the Wire Feed; if not fully inserted the gas connection will leak, preventing shielding gas from reaching the arc. NOTICE: do not overtighten the Knob.
- Wire Feed Control Cable connects to the Wire Feed Control Socket inside the machine; plug fits the socket in one specific orientation only.
- Gas Hose on Spool Gun Cable Connector connects to the Spool Gun Gas Outlet on the front of the Welder; see paragraph 17 on page 14 for shielding gas setup.
- Refer to the Spool Gun manual for operational information.

## Owner Manual Page 18

**Content:** Start of "Basic Wire Welding" section. Safety warnings (required PPE, arc eye/skin damage, fume hazards, burn hazards), an overview of what each wire process welds (flux-cored vs. MIG vs. aluminum with Spool Gun), and guidance to practice on scrap first.

**Tables:** None.

**Diagrams & images:**
- **Four PPE/hazard pictograms (right column, top to bottom):** (1) triangular warning sign showing arc rays striking a welder's head/eyes (arc flash hazard); (2) circle icon of welding gloves; (3) circle icon of a full-face respirator/mask; (4) triangular warning sign showing a figure with fumes/inhalation hazard (do not breathe arc fumes).
- **"Keep tip clear of grounded objects" drawing (middle right):** Front view of the VULCAN welder with "Power Switch" label pointing at the switch; an equals sign ("=") to a MIG Gun whose tip emits an electric arc flash symbol — illustrating that the gun tip is electrically live whenever the unit is plugged in and the Power Switch is on.
- **Boxed reminder (bottom right):** "Practice your welding technique on scrap pieces before welding anything of value."
- WARNING banners: a black triangle-with-exclamation icon next to "Read the ENTIRE IMPORTANT SAFETY INFORMATION section…"; a "WARNING TO PREVENT SERIOUS INJURY:" block; a "WARNING TO PREVENT SERIOUS INJURY, FIRE AND BURNS:" block with a flame warning triangle.
- JSON manifest lists no extracted image files for this page.

**Key facts:**
- Required PPE: minimum shade number 10 full face shield (or welding mask), ear protection, welding gloves, sleeves and apron, NIOSH-approved respirator, and fire resistant work clothes without pockets.
- Arc light can cause permanent damage to eyes and skin; do not breathe arc fumes.
- Flux-cored wire welding welds mild steel and stainless steel WITHOUT shielding gas.
- MIG welding uses solid wire and shielding gas; welds mild steel and stainless steel; can weld thinner workpieces than flux-cored.
- Aluminum welding requires the optional Spool Gun (sold separately) with aluminum wire and shielding gas.
- Practice periods recommended whenever welding: a different thickness of material, a different type of material, a different type of connection, or using a different process.
- WARNING: Keep welding tip clear of grounded objects whenever unit is plugged in and turned on.

## Owner Manual Page 19

**Content:** "Duty Cycle (Duration of Use)" — definition of duty cycle, thermal protection behavior, and the rated duty cycle charts for 120 VAC and 240 VAC input. Then "Setting Up The Weld" steps 1–3: practice on scrap, clamp/chamfer workpieces, and attach the ground clamp to bare metal.

**Tables:**

**Rated Duty Cycle — 120VAC (boxed chart, top right):**

| Rated Duty Cycle 120VAC — 40% Use at 100A — For 10 Continuous Minutes | |
|---|---|
| Welding | 4 Minutes |
| Resting | 6 Minutes |
| Continuous use | 100% Continuous Use at 75A |

**Rated Duty Cycle — 240VAC (boxed chart, middle right):**

| Rated Duty Cycle 240VAC — 25% Use at 200A — For 10 Continuous Minutes | |
|---|---|
| Welding | 2-1/2 Minutes |
| Resting | 7-1/2 Minutes |
| Continuous use | 100% Continuous Use at 115A |

**Diagrams & images:**
- **Two duty-cycle clock charts:** each chart pairs the text above with a circular 10-minute clock face; the shaded slice represents the welding portion (4 min on the 120VAC chart, 2-1/2 min on the 240VAC chart) and the remainder the resting portion.
- **Workpiece clamping diagram (left, under "Setting Up The Weld"):** Two workpieces held edge-to-edge by clamps at both ends. Labels: "clamps" (top), "workpieces", "Clean surfaces to bare metal." (pointing to the joint area), and "Chamfer thick workpieces." with a small inset cross-section showing beveled/chamfered edges of a thick joint.
- **Ground clamp diagram (bottom right):** Welder front view with Ground Clamp connected; callouts "Workpiece", "Ground Clamp", "Clean surface to bare metal." Note beneath the welder: "Ground connection depends on desired welding polarity".
- Extracted image `p19-x125.jpeg` (223×223) is a gray gradient fill graphic (decorative shading, no informational content).

**Key facts:**
- Duty Cycle = number of minutes within a 10-minute period a welder can produce a given current without overheating. Example: 40% duty cycle at 100A → weld 4 minutes, rest at least 6 minutes.
- 120VAC rated duty cycle: 40% at 100A (4 min weld / 6 min rest per 10 min); 100% continuous use at 75A.
- 240VAC rated duty cycle: 25% at 200A (2-1/2 min weld / 7-1/2 min rest per 10 min); 100% continuous use at 115A.
- Internal thermal protection: on overheat the welder shuts down automatically and a warning screen appears in the LCD Display; it returns to service after cooling off.
- If overheated: rest MIG Gun on an electrically non-conductive, heat-proof surface (e.g. concrete slab) well clear of the ground clamp; leave Power Switch ON so the internal Fan cools the Welder; afterwards use shorter welding / longer rest periods.
- Clean weld surfaces to bare metal with wire brush or angle grinder — no rust, paint, oil, or other materials.
- Use clamps (not included); control gap between workpieces; chamfer (bevel) edges of thicker workpieces for full penetration.
- Vehicle welding: disconnect vehicle battery power from BOTH the positive connection and the ground before welding to protect electrical systems/electronics from high voltage and high frequency bursts.
- Clamp Ground Cable to bare metal on the workpiece near the weld area, or to the metal work bench; ground connection depends on desired welding polarity.

- Auto Weld Settings: Left Knob adjusts Wire Feed Speed (Amperage); Right Knob adjusts Voltage (screen example: 121 in/min WFS, 13.8 V).
- Run screen status bar shows Process (MIG Steel), Gas Type (C25), Wire Diameter (.030"), Material Thickness (24Ga), plus Run-In WFS, Inductance, and Spot Timer shortcuts.
- White mark on the adjustment line = recommended setting for the selected wire/electrode diameter and workpiece thickness.

**Key facts:**
- Optional Settings navigation: press Main Control Knob to enter; turn knob until desired setting appears; press to select; turn to adjust.
- Run-In WFS — adjusts wire speed before it contacts the workpiece; value is a % of the pre-set wire feed speed (WFS).
- Inductance — adjusts length of arc; increase for more fluid puddle and flatter bead; decrease for colder puddle.
- Spot Timer — sets timer for spot welding.
- Recall Setting — retrieves saved settings.
- Save Setting — programs up to 5 different configurations (5 slots); overwrite via OVERWRITE MEMORY SETTING screen, choose YES, press Main Control Knob to save.
- Note: initial settings may need adjustment after stopping and inspecting the weld.
- DANGER (asphyxiation): do not open gas without proper ventilation; fix gas leaks immediately; shielding gas can displace air causing rapid loss of consciousness and death; shielding gas WITHOUT carbon dioxide is even more hazardous because asphyxiation can start without feeling shortness of breath.
- Gas start-up: (1) open gas cylinder valve all the way; (2) set Flow Gauge to the SCFH value indicated on the Settings Chart.

## Owner Manual Page 22

**Content:** "Basic Wire Welding Technique" steps 1–6: igniting the arc, stringer vs. weave beads, gun angles for butt and fillet joints, push angle (MIG/solid wire), drag angle (flux-cored), and CTWD (Contact Tip to Work Distance).

**Tables:** None.

**Diagrams & images:**
- **"stringer bead" / "weave bead" drawings (top right):** Two side-by-side illustrations of weld beads on plate. Stringer bead: wire drawn in a steady straight line, single-row rippled bead with a straight direction arrow. Weave bead: wire moved back and forth across the joint, wider bead with a zig-zag/weave motion arrow.
- **"Weld MIG Gun angles, viewed from front of weld joint" (middle right):** Two drawings. Left: "fillet weld joint" — MIG Gun aimed into the corner of a T-shaped (vertical+horizontal plate) joint at "45°". Right: "butt weld joint" — MIG Gun perpendicular ("90°", straight up and down) into the joint between two flat plates.
- **"Push Angle" and "Drag Angle" drawings (lower middle):** Push Angle (left, captioned "Solid Wire with Shielding Gas"): gun tilted "0-15°" so the wire points AWAY from the "Weld Direction" arrow (tilted opposite the direction of travel). Drag Angle (right, captioned "Flux-Cored Wire without Gas"): gun tilted "0-15°" so the wire points IN the "Weld Direction" of travel (trailing tilt).
- **CTWD drawing (bottom right):** MIG Gun nozzle over a weld bead with callout "CTWD (up to 1/2")" indicating the distance from contact tip to work surface.
- Extracted images `p22-x477.jpeg` (124×122) and `p22-x154.jpeg` (183×131) are gray gradient fill graphics (decorative shading for the bead illustrations, no informational content).

**Key facts:**
- Ignite arc: press (and hold) Trigger and contact the area to be welded with the electrode wire.
- Stringer bead = steady straight-line draw for narrow welds; weave bead = back-and-forth across the joint for wider welds (takes practice).
- Gun angle: 90° (straight up and down) for butt (end-to-end) welds; 45° for fillet (T-shaped) welds — wire directed straight into the joint.
- Push angle (MIG solid wire with shielding gas): gun tilted anywhere between straight on and 15° AWAY from the direction of welding.
- Drag angle (flux-cored wire without shielding gas): gun tilted anywhere between straight on and 15° IN the direction of welding.
- CTWD (Contact Tip to Work Distance): Contact Tip must remain within 1/2" of the work surface.

- Step 12 (MIG ONLY): close shielding gas cylinder valve securely, remove Regulator and replace cap, disconnect Gas Hose from Welder, store and secure gas cylinder.
