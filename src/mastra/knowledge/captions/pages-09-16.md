## Owner's Manual Page 10
**Content:** Start of the "MIG/Flux-Cored Wire Welding" section: safety warnings, and steps 1–5 of Wire Spool Installation / Wire Setup — powering off, opening the door, and installing a 1–2 lb wire spool.

**Tables:** None.

**Diagrams & images:**
- Step 1 illustration: front view of the welder showing the **Power Switch** on the front panel, plus a power plug being unplugged from a wall receptacle (curved arrow showing removal).
- Step 2 illustration: side view of the closed welder cabinet with labels **Door** (the side access panel) and **Door Latch** (the latch near the bottom of the door).
- "1-2 lb Spool Loading" — exploded/stacked assembly diagram of the small spool mounting, from bottom to top: **Welder Wall** (base), **Spool Brake Pad**, **1-2 lb Wire Spool** sitting on the **Spool Spindle**, **Spacer** over the spindle, and **Wingnut** on top to secure the stack. All parts labeled with leader lines.

**Key facts:**
- Turn the Power Switch OFF and unplug the Welder before setup; remove the protective foam and cardboard from the Welder before setup.
- Pull up on the Door Latch to open the Door.
- For a 1–2 lb spool: remove Wingnut and Spacer; if replacing, remove the old spool and all remaining wire from the liners.
- Place the new spool over the Spool Spindle against the Spool Brake Pad; the spool must unwind **clockwise**.
- Secure with Spacer + Wingnut. If the spool can spin freely, the Wingnut is too loose — wire will unravel/unspool, causing tangling and feeding problems.

- Step 11 illustration: two-panel before/after drawing of the wire feed mechanism. Left panel: **Feed Tensioner** (knob) with a curved arrow showing counterclockwise loosening; label **Idler Arm**. Arrow between panels. Right panel: the spring-loaded Idler Arm swung up/open after tension is released.

**Key facts:**
- 10–12 lb spool installation requires the **10-12 lb Spool Adapter**: place the adapter over the Spool Spindle against the Spool Brake Pad, then place the spool over the adapter and line up the pin on the adapter with the hole in the spool.
- Spool must unwind **clockwise**; secure with Spacer + Wingnut (same "too loose = unravel/tangle" warning as small spools).
- Screw the Spool Knob into the Spool Adapter after the spool is secured (step 10).
- Turn the Feed Tensioner knob **counterclockwise** to loosen, pull it down to remove tension; the spring-loaded Idler Arm moves up.

## Owner's Manual Page 12
**Content:** Step 12: Feed Roller instructions — verifying/replacing the feed roller to match wire type (solid core vs. flux-cored) and wire diameter.

**Tables:** None.

**Diagrams & images:**
- Four-step (A–D) sequence diagram of the wire feed mechanism, connected by large arrows labeled A, B, C, D:
  - **A**: close-up of the feed mechanism with the **Feed Roller Knob** labeled; arrow indicates unscrewing the knob counterclockwise.
  - **B**: the mechanism with the knob removed and the **Feed Roller** exposed (callout points at the roller).
  - **C**: mechanism with the roller flipped/replaced.
  - **D**: mechanism with the knob screwed back on to secure the roller.
- Feed roller groove chart (two roller side-view drawings with labeled grooves):
  - **Solid Core V-Groove** roller: one side labeled "0.030/0.035 groove", other side labeled "0.025 groove".
  - **Flux-Cored Knurled Groove** roller: one side labeled "0.045 groove", other side labeled "0.030/0.035 groove".

**Key facts:**
- Feed roller must match both the wire type and the wire diameter marked on the spool: **Solid Core = V-Groove roller; Flux-Cored = Knurled Groove roller**.
- Solid Core V-Groove roller groove sizes: **0.030/0.035** and **0.025**.
- Flux-Cored Knurled Groove roller sizes: **0.045** and **0.030/0.035**.
- Feed Roller Knob unscrews **counterclockwise**; the number showing on the installed roller must equal the wire diameter on the spool.

## Owner's Manual Page 13
**Content:** Steps 13–16: connecting the MIG gun cable connector and wire feed control cable to the wire feed mechanism, and DCEN polarity setup for flux-cored (gasless) welding.

**Tables:** None.

**Diagrams & images:**
- Step 13/14 illustration (top): gun cable being inserted into the wire feed mechanism. Labels: **Wire Feed Mechanism**, **Knob** (the locking knob on the mechanism), **Gun Cable Connector**. Caption below: "Incorrect—Connector not fully inserted" (gap visible between connector and socket).
- Step 15 illustration (middle): same area with the connector fully seated; labels **Wire Feed Control Cable** and the connector; caption below: "Correct—Connector fully inserted".
- "DCEN Flux-Cored (Gasless) Polarity Setup" — large drawing of the welder front panel with the MIG gun and ground clamp connected. Labels: **Wire Feed Power Cable in Negative Socket** (upper socket) and **Ground Clamp Cable in Positive Socket** (lower socket).

**Key facts:**
- Loosen the Knob on the wire feed mechanism, insert the Gun Cable Connector through the hole in the welder front into the socket on the wire feed, ensure it is **fully inserted**, then tighten the Knob securely. If not fully inserted, the gas connection leaks and shielding gas will not reach the arc. Do **not** overtighten the Knob.
- Wire Feed Control Cable: insert through the hole in the welder front, connect to the Wire Feed Control Socket inside the machine, tighten the lock ring; the plug fits the socket in **one specific orientation only**.
- **DCEN (Direct Current Electrode Negative) for flux-cored (gasless) welding: Ground Clamp Cable → Positive (+) Socket; Wire Feed Power Cable → Negative (–) Socket.** Twist cables clockwise all the way to lock in place.

## Owner's Manual Page 15
**Content:** Steps 18–23: preparing and feeding the welding wire — cutting the wire end, guiding wire into the inlet liner, setting feed tension, and removing the MIG gun nozzle and contact tip.

**Tables:** None.

**Diagrams & images:**
- Top box (IMPORTANT, bold-bordered): "Securely hold onto the end of the welding wire and keep tension on it during the following steps. If this is not done, the welding wire will unravel and unspool which can cause tangling and feeding problems."
- Steps 18–19 illustration: a hand holding the **Welding Wire** (labels: "HOLD WIRE SECURELY", "Welding Wire", **Wire Spool**) guiding it into the **Wire Inlet Liner** and **Feed Guide** of the feed mechanism (both labeled).
- Step 20 illustration: two-panel before/after of the feed mechanism — **Idler Arm** pushed down and **Feed Tensioner** swung up to latch across the tip of the arm (both labeled; arrow between panels).
- Steps 21–22 illustration: **MIG Gun** close-up labeling **Nozzle** (front, pulled off) and **Contact Tip** (under the nozzle, unscrewed counterclockwise).
- Bottom box (IMPORTANT, bold-bordered): "Stainless steel wire is less flexible than other welding wire. Therefore, it is more difficult to feed through the liner and gun. It is especially important to keep the gun cable straight while feeding stainless steel wire."

**Key facts:**
- Cut off all bent/crimped wire; the cut end must have **no burrs or sharp edges**.
- Guide **at least 12 inches** of wire into the Wire Inlet Liner and Feed Guide while keeping tension on the wire.
- **Feed Tensioner setting: 3–5 for solid wire; 2–3 for flux-cored wire.** Too much force on flux-cored wire will crush it and cause feeding issues.
- Remove the Nozzle by pulling it off; remove the Contact Tip by unscrewing **counterclockwise**.
- Lay the MIG Gun Cable out in a **straight line** so wire feeds easily; leave the cover open to observe the feed mechanism.
- Stainless steel wire is less flexible — keep the gun cable especially straight when feeding it.

**Key facts:**

- Do not touch the Gun's Trigger while powering on or feeding wire.
- Point the gun away from all objects; press and hold the **Cold Wire Feed Switch** until wire feeds through **two inches (2″)**.
- The wire liner may come out with the welding wire — this is normal; push it back into the gun.
- If wire does not feed and the spool is stationary: turn OFF, unplug, and slightly tighten the Feed Tensioner **clockwise** before retrying.
