**Key facts:**
- This quick-start is for the Vulcan OmniPro 220, Harbor Freight item **57812**.
- Ships configured for a **2 lb wire spool**; an **adapter and knob are required for a 10–12 lb spool**.
- Wire must pass through **both feed guides** before clamping into the feed mechanism.
- See the full manual if **changing wire size or using solid wire** (the guide covers flux wire setup only on this page).
- Wire feed tension knob: tighten **clockwise** incrementally until the wire **bends from feed tension at 2″–3″** against a wood block.
- "**2″**" callout appears at the MIG gun tip during the step 3 cold-feed procedure.
- Cold wire feeding is done with nozzle and contact tip **removed**, then the welder is **unplugged** before replacing nozzle and tip (step 5).
- Cable connections for flux wire welding are on the **back of the guide** (page 2).
- Warnings: read manual warnings before use; keep torch and wire clear of grounded objects while the welder is plugged in.

**Key facts:**
- **STICK (SMAW):** ground clamp → **NEGATIVE (−)** terminal; electrode holder → **POSITIVE (+)** terminal (DCEP); wire feed power left disconnected.
- **MIG (GMAW):** ground clamp → **NEGATIVE (−)** terminal; wire feed power → **POSITIVE (+)** terminal (DCEP); MIG gun connects to the wire feed; wire feed (gun trigger) control connects **inside** the welder.
- **FLUX-CORED (FCAW):** same as MIG but polarity **reversed**: ground clamp → **POSITIVE (+)**, wire feed power → **NEGATIVE (−)** (DCEN).
- **TIG (GTAW):** ground clamp → **POSITIVE (+)** terminal; TIG torch → **NEGATIVE (−)** terminal (DCEN); gas line connects to the gas regulator; foot pedal connects **inside** the welder; wire feed power left disconnected.
- Two connections go **inside the welder** (not on the front terminals): the MIG wire feed control and the TIG foot pedal.

**Key facts:**
- Thickness capability by process: FCAW **18 gauge to 5/16″**; MIG **22 gauge to 3/8″**; Stick **10 gauge to 1/2″**; TIG **24 gauge to 3/16″**.
- Aluminum: MIG requires a **spool gun**; TIG welding aluminum/magnesium alloys requires **AC TIG**; TIG on steel/stainless/chrome moly requires **DC TIG**.
- Gas: MIG and TIG require shielding gas (indoor welding recommended); FCAW and Stick require no gas and suit outdoor/windy conditions.
- Skill level: FCAW and MIG = LOW (MIG "Easiest to learn"); Stick = MODERATE; TIG = HIGH.
- Input voltage check: determine whether you have **120 VOLT or 240 VOLT** before choosing.
- Duty cycle definition: minutes of welding within a **10-minute period** at the specified current without overheating. Example given: **165 A @ 30% duty cycle = 3 minutes welding / 7 minutes resting**.
- MIG uses solid wire + shielding gas and suits thin material/sheet metal; Flux-Cored uses cored wire, needs minimal surface prep, welds over mill scale/rust, works outdoors in wind, and gives deep penetration over material gaps.
