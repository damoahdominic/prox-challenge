## Owner Manual Page 26
**Content:** TIG torch maintenance and assembly: how to sharpen a tungsten electrode (grinding technique, tip geometry) and how to assemble the TIG torch (collet body, collet, ceramic nozzle, electrode, back cap). Both tungsten electrode and TIG torch are sold separately.

**Tables:** None.

**Diagrams & images:**
- Grinding illustration (no extracted image file; vector art, upper right): an Electrode held at an angle against a "Grinding Wheel" (labels "Electrode" and "Grinding Wheel"). Two small circular insets below show the correct blunt-point tip (plain circle) and an incorrect tip (circle with a prohibition/"no" slash through it).
- TIG torch assembly diagram — extracted image `/manual/owner-manual/images/p26-x187.jpeg` (545×612): exploded/diagrammatic view of the TIG Torch with labels "Back Cap" (top), "Collet", "Collet Body", "Ceramic Nozzle" (front/bottom), and the callout "Insert tungsten Electrode here." with an arrow curving to the front opening of the torch.

**Key facts:**
- Dedicate a fine grit grinding wheel EXCLUSIVELY to electrode grinding to avoid contamination.
- Wear a respirator and ANSI-approved safety goggles when grinding — some electrodes contain hazardous additives.
- Pull the electrode out from the FRONT of the torch; pulling from the rear damages the Collet and creates burrs.
- Snap off dulled/contaminated electrode ends by gripping above the contaminated section with pliers.
- Grinding direction must be parallel to the length of the electrode; rotate the tip until a blunt point forms.
- Ideal tip: conical portion 2-1/2 times as long as the electrode diameter.
- Electrode must protrude 1/8"–1/4" beyond the Ceramic Nozzle; lock in place with the Back Cap.
- Assembly order: Collet Body threads into front of torch → Ceramic Nozzle onto Collet Body → Collet inserted into back of torch into Collet Body → Tungsten Electrode into Collet from the front → Back Cap locks it.
- Collet and Collet Body sizes must match the Tungsten Electrode size; electrode size comes from the Settings Chart on top of the welder (based on material thickness).

## Owner Manual Page 28
**Content:** Introduction to basic TIG/Stick welding: required protective gear, which process welds which material (DC TIG, AC TIG, Stick), and the recommendation to practice on scrap first.

**Tables:** None.

**Diagrams & images:**
- Four PPE/hazard pictograms in a 2×2 arrangement (no extracted image files; vector symbols): top-left, triangular warning sign showing a welder struck by arc rays/sparks (arc flash hazard); top-right, grey circle with welding gloves; bottom-left, grey circle with a head wearing a respirator mask; bottom-right, triangular warning sign showing electric shock/electrocution hazard.
- Warning box (top of page): triangle-with-exclamation icon next to "Read the ENTIRE IMPORTANT SAFETY INFORMATION section at the beginning of this manual including all text under subheadings therein before set up or use of this product."
- "Power On" illustration (no extracted image file; vector art): line drawing of the welder's front panel with callout "Power On" pointing at it, an equals sign "=", then a TIG torch/ electrode with a lightning-bolt flash at its tip — meaning whenever the unit is powered on, the torch/holder is live.
- Fire warning: black WARNING banner with flame-in-triangle icon: "TO PREVENT SERIOUS INJURY, FIRE AND BURNS: Keep welding tip clear of grounded objects whenever unit is plugged in and turned on."
- Boxed note: "Practice your welding technique on scrap pieces before welding anything of value."

**Key facts:**
- DC TIG welding: mild steel and stainless steel, using a TIG Rod and shielding gas.
- AC TIG welding: aluminum, using a TIG Rod and shielding gas.
- Stick welding: mild steel and stainless steel, using a Stick Electrode WITHOUT shielding gas.
- Minimum PPE: shade number 10 (minimum) full face shield or welding mask, ear protection, welding gloves, sleeves and apron, NIOSH-approved respirator, fire-resistant work clothes without pockets.
- Arc light can cause permanent eye and skin damage; do not breathe arc fumes.
- Practice first whenever welding a different thickness of material, a different type of material, a different type of connection, or using a different process.

## Owner Manual Page 29
**Content:** Duty cycle explanation (definition, thermal protection behavior) with TIG and Stick rated duty-cycle tables for 240VAC and 120VAC, followed by "Setting up the Weld" — practice welds, cleaning to bare metal, clamping/chamfering workpieces, vehicle-battery precaution, and ground clamp placement.

**Diagrams & images:**
- Four pie-chart clock icons inside the duty-cycle tables (extracted images `/manual/owner-manual/images/p29-x217.jpeg`, `p29-x220.jpeg`, `p29-x224.jpeg`, `p29-x231.jpeg`, `p29-x240.jpeg`, `p29-x246.jpeg`, `p29-x252.jpeg`, `p29-x256.jpeg` — eight small files making up the four clock graphics): each clock shows a 10-minute dial with the welding portion shaded (3, 4, 2-1/2, and 4 minutes respectively) and labels "Minutes Welding" (left) and "Minutes Resting" (right).
- "Setting up the Weld" illustration (no extracted image file; vector art): two workpieces held together by clamps from both sides; labels "clamps", "workpieces", "Clean surfaces to bare metal." (pointing at the joint), and "Chamfer thick workpieces." (showing a small beveled-edge detail).
- Ground clamp illustration (no extracted image file; vector art, bottom right): front view of the welder with the ground cable running to a Ground Clamp attached to a Workpiece; labels "Workpiece", "Ground Clamp", "Clean surface to bare metal.", and caption "Ground connection depends on desired welding polarity".

**Key facts:**
- Duty cycle = number of minutes within a 10-minute period the welder can produce a given current without overheating. Example given: 40% duty cycle at 125A → rest at least 6 minutes after every 4 minutes of continuous welding.
- TIG: 240VAC 30% @ 175A (3 min weld / 7 min rest), 100% @ 105A; 120VAC 40% @ 125A (4 min weld / 6 min rest), 100% @ 90A.
- Stick: 240VAC 25% @ 175A (2-1/2 min weld / 7-1/2 min rest), 100% @ 100A; 120VAC 40% @ 80A (4 min weld / 6 min rest), 100% @ 60A.
- On overheat, the welder shuts down automatically and a warning screen appears on the LCD; it returns to service after cooling. Rest the TIG Torch or Electrode Holder on an electrically non-conductive, heat-proof surface (e.g., concrete slab), well clear of the ground clamp.
- Allow cooling with the Power Switch ON so the internal fan runs.
- Weld surfaces must be cleaned to bare metal (wire brush or angle grinder) — no rust, paint, or oil.
- Thicker workpiece edges may need chamfering/beveling for full penetration.
- NOTICE (vehicle welding): disconnect the vehicle battery from BOTH the positive connection and the ground before welding, to protect vehicle electronics from high voltage and high frequency bursts.
- Clamp the Ground Cable to bare metal on the workpiece near the weld area, or to the metal work bench where the workpiece is clamped.

- Process selection: Power Switch ON → press Home Button → turn Main Control Knob until TIG appears → press Main Control Knob to select. Press Main Control Knob for next screen; press Back Button for previous screen.

- DANGER (asphyxiation): do not open gas without proper ventilation; fix leaks immediately; shielding gas without carbon dioxide can asphyxiate without any feeling of shortness of breath.
- Do not weld without the Grounding Clamp; set the torch on a nonconductive, nonflammable surface when not held; hold the TIG Rod only with an electrically insulated welding glove.

## Owner Manual Page 31
**Content:** TIG welding continued: optional settings (Recall/Save), then the actual welding technique — steps 10–19 covering electrode distance, arc initiation (foot pedal or lift), torch angle, adding rod, shutdown, and post-use storage.

**Tables:** None.

**Diagrams & images:**
- "Recall Setting" LCD screen — extracted image `/manual/owner-manual/images/p31-x270.png`: screen titled "Recall Setting" with a large upload-style icon (arrow pointing up out of a tray); below the screen, two small select icons (up-arrow tray highlighted, down-arrow tray).
- "Save Setting" LCD screen — extracted image `/manual/owner-manual/images/p31-x272.png`: screen titled "Save Setting" with a large download-style icon (arrow pointing down into a tray); below the screen, the down-arrow tray icon is highlighted.
- Boxed note (no image): "After practice welding on scrap, stop, and check your progress. Perform Strike Test according to Strike Test on page 34. After making any necessary adjustments, continue to weld while carefully following the DUTY CYCLE guidelines as explained on page 29."

**Key facts:**
- Optional Settings navigation: press Main Control Knob to enter, turn to desired setting, press to select, turn to adjust.
- Recall Setting = retrieve saved settings; Save Setting = program up to 5 different configurations.
- Maintain a constant distance between the Tungsten Electrode and the workpiece: between 1 and 1.5 times the diameter of the Electrode.
- Open the valve on the TIG Torch to start gas flow BEFORE initiating the arc.
- Arc initiation: with Foot Pedal — push pedal, touch Electrode to workpiece and lift; without Foot Pedal — touch Electrode to workpiece and lift.
- Torch angle once the puddle is hot: tilt backward about 10–15 degrees from vertical, move back slightly; add TIG Rod material to the FRONT end of the weld puddle.
- Remove the TIG Rod each time the Electrode is advanced, but keep it inside the gas shield to prevent oxidation contamination.
- Finishing: release Foot Pedal but keep the Torch on the puddle until the weld solidifies; without pedal, pull the Torch away until the arc breaks, then return gas coverage until the weld solidifies.
- Shutdown: close torch valve, turn Right Knob to OFF, set torch on nonconductive/nonflammable surface, turn Power Switch OFF.
- Metal work bench MUST be grounded when TIG welding.
- After use: cool down, unplug, remove ground clamp, disconnect TIG Torch/Ground/Foot Pedal cables, close cylinder valve and replace cap, disconnect gas hose, secure cylinder, store indoors out of children's reach.
